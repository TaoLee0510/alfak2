# Pilot analysis rules fixed before design-risk comparisons

The two workers produce 60 cases: twelve paired biological units on six
independent landscapes, five designs per unit. The primary equal-cell-budget
comparisons are time600 and composition_anchor600 against depth600. control300
quantifies the gain from doubled typing depth. composition600 is the disclosed
unanchored-support diagnostic, not a replacement winner selected by its result.

Use baseline-control actual-distance groups as the primary comparison and
report the planned-stencil and native-distance views separately. Average
biological replicates within landscape, landscapes within family, then families
equally. Show all six landscape differences and three family ratios. Do not
treat twelve biological units as twelve independent landscapes. With two
landscapes per family, descriptive pilot ratios do not certify significance,
noninferiority, coverage or a final-test sample size.

Any failed fit, incomplete prediction/interval, missing expected case or
nonfinite primary risk invalidates the complete comparison. Do not use
finite-mean aggregation to remove it. Descriptive rank/amplitude metrics may
be undefined for small or constant strata; show their available denominators.

Report separate triage flags: point risk nonworse at all original distances,
5% centered-shape benefit at all distances (the planning target), edge risk
nonworse, proper interval scores nonworse at 50/80/95%, and every family/distance
RMSE/shape/edge/IS95 ratio <=1.05. These are development decisions, not formal
tests. A design is called promising for the all-distance objective only if it
meets the conjunction and complete availability. A large far-field gain with
near-field harm remains a tradeoff, not a global improvement. No reserved
calibration/test data are generated by this analysis. Larger-domain follow-up
requires a credible design and a separate resource/validation checkpoint.
